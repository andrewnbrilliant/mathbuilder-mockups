Scenario

One question shell. The learner chooses the family of model before building anything, and that choice rewrites the template, the solver and the closing sentence.

The four moves

1 Choose — linear, quadratic or exponential. Nothing else is on screen yet, so the decision is made from the words of the scenario alone.

2 Fill — the template they picked appears, all blanks at once, then the value of y the question is asking about, then the equation that follows.

3 Solve — a transformer workspace whose pills suit the form they chose, then the final-answer blanks that are actually graded.

4 Conclude — interpret the answer in the scenario's own words, and only then see the graph.

Notes

The model blank rewrites the rest of the question — template, pill set, how many answer blanks there are, and which closing sentence is asked. Picking the wrong family is never a dead end: the learner gets that template, fills it, solves it, and the argument fails in the mathematics on Check.

The workspace is seeded from the learner's own filled-in template, not from the right answer. Model a draining tank as y = 4x + 60 and the solver hands you 4x + 60 = 0 and cheerfully solves it to x = −15.

Nothing hints at why a solution might not belong. On the quadratic branch both roots are offered as toggles and either, both or one can be kept — the learner has to notice that a negative time is before the throw.

The graph appears only at the very end. Shown earlier it would give away the family; shown last it ties the answer to the curve — and it plots the real situation while marking the answers the learner actually committed to.

Template blanks are all visible at once — an expression reads better whole — while the sentence blanks in stage 4 still arrive one at a time.

The solve workspace

Each branch loads a different transformer. All three share Add, Subtract, Multiply by, Divide by, Combine; the quadratic adds Factor, Split cases, Square root; the exponential adds Take log and Raise to power.

Add and Subtract do not tidy up after themselves. They leave −4x + 60 − 60 = 0 − 60 on the page and you have to hit Combine; that is the step most solvers hide.

The intended exponential route is ÷ 50 → Take log base 2. It runs real algebra, not a script — any legal move is allowed and illegal ones are refused with a reason ("Take a log once the power is on its own", "That would leave a fraction").

The workspace is scaffolding; the final-answer blanks are the commitment. Nothing is auto-filled from the working-out.
