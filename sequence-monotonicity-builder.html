Why evidence, not just a label

Naming a sequence increasing is a guess until it is tied to a claim about every consecutive pair.

“Neither” is the interesting answer: it is true for two quite different reasons, and the learner has to say which one they mean before they can produce the evidence.

Notes

The depth of the argument is chosen by the learner. Two blanks settle increasing or decreasing; “neither” costs a third and sometimes a fourth, because that claim genuinely needs more evidence.

The counterexample pair is graded by rule, not by an answer key. Any two comparisons that are both true and point in opposite directions are accepted — there are several honest ways to show a sequence turns around, and none of them is the “expected” one.

Every tile bank is generated from the computed terms, with the false direction of each pair kept in as a distractor. Editing a rule can never leave a stale tile behind.

A wrong first choice still finishes. Call a decreasing sequence increasing and you are handed the same evidence sentence, build a complete argument, and it fails on Check — with the red box on the claim that actually broke.
