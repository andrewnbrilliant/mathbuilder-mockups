Scenario

One question shell. Two providers, a fixed fee and a per-unit rate each — the learner models the pair, solves the system, and then says which provider wins on each side of the crossing.

The three moves

1 Set up — turn each sentence into c = rate × amount + fee. Both equations are built at once; an equation reads badly half-finished.

2 Solve — a SystemOfEquationsTransformer-style workspace to do the algebra in, then the two final-answer blanks that are actually graded.

3 Interpret — the crossing is a price and an amount, and it splits the number line into a region each provider owns.

The interpret stage is where the algebra stops being a pair of numbers: d is the break-even amount and c is the break-even price, and the sign of the rate difference decides who owns each side of it.

Notes

Stage 1's four blanks are all visible at once — an equation reads badly half-built — while the sentence blanks in stage 3 still arrive one at a time.

The rate blanks come before the fee blanks in tap order, so the learner has to decide which number is "per km" before the easy one.

The graph appears only at the very end. Shown earlier it gives away the crossing; shown last it turns "longer trips" and "shorter trips" into two shaded regions you can point at.

The solve workspace

Stage 2 is a small SystemOfEquationsTransformer: rows A and B, a bank of action pills, a step row per transformation, and Undo. Instead of dragging a pill onto a row, tap a row to make it active, then tap a pill.

The intended route is Substitute A into B → Subtract d → Subtract 3 → Substitute B into A → Combine. Add and Subtract take a constant or a variable term, so −d is a legal move.

It runs real algebra, not a script — any legal move is allowed, and illegal ones are refused with a reason ("That would leave fractions", "Substitute needs the other equation to have a single variable on its own").

The workspace is scaffolding; the final-answer blanks are the commitment. Nothing is auto-filled from the working-out.
