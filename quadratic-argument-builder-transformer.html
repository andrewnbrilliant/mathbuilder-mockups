Scenario

One question shell. The learner's own count of the solutions decides how many roots they are asked for, and which interpretation sentence closes the argument.

The four moves

1 Model — substitute into h = −5t² + v0t + h0 and say what height the question is asking about.

2 Count — the discriminant, then how many real solutions it implies.

3 Solve — a QuadraticEquationTransformer-style workspace to do the algebra in, then as many final-answer blanks as they just claimed there are.

4 Interpret — decide which solutions the situation allows, and answer in words.

Notes

The count blank rewrites the rest of the question — claim one solution and only one root blank ever appears. Wrong counts still produce a complete, coherent argument that fails on Check, rather than a dead end.

The keep-or-drop step only exists on the two-solution branch, which is where it is the actual mathematical work. Nothing is asked twice.

Nothing hints at why a solution might not belong. Both times are offered as toggles and either, both or one can be kept — the learner has to notice that a negative time is before the throw, rather than being told so in the sentence they are completing.

The graph appears only at stage 4. Shown earlier it would give away the count; shown at the end it ties the dropped root to the shaded "before the throw" region — and it marks whichever roots the learner actually kept, so it never pre-empts the decision.

Equation blanks are all visible at once — an expression reads better whole — while sentence blanks still arrive one at a time.

The solve workspace

Stage 3 is a small QuadraticEquationTransformer: action pills, a step row per transformation, split cases into two tracks, and Undo. The intended route here is ÷ −5 → Factor → Split cases → +/− on each case.

It runs real algebra, not a script — any legal move is allowed, and illegal ones are refused with a reason ("Factor once the t² coefficient is 1", "That would leave fractions").

The workspace is scaffolding; the final-answer blanks are the commitment. Nothing is auto-filled from the working-out, so the argument above and below stays intact — and a learner who claims two solutions for an equation that has none watches Factor refuse, which is the lesson.
